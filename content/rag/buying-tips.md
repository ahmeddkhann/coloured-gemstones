# Buying coloured gemstones — tips

## The four Cs (adapted for colour)
For coloured stones, colour usually matters most, then clarity, cut, and carat. A vivid, well-cut smaller stone often looks more valuable than a larger dull one.

## Colour
Look for even colour, pleasing saturation, and life under both daylight and indoor light. Hue names (e.g. sapphire, ruby, emerald, tourmaline) describe type; the catalogue also lists colour for each stone.

## Clarity
Eye-clean stones are prized, but some inclusions are normal and expected (especially in emerald). Ask what is visible without magnification.

## Cut and carat
Cut shapes how light returns and how large a stone appears for its weight. Carat is weight, not millimetre size — two stones of equal carat can look different depending on cut depth.

## Origin
Origin can influence rarity and price (for example Kashmir sapphire or Colombian emerald lore). Catalogue listings include origin when known; treat it as part of the story, not a guarantee of quality alone.

## Certificates
If a laboratory report is important to you, ask the store before ordering whether a specific piece has paperwork. Do not assume every listing includes a certificate unless stated.
